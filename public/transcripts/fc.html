● # AirHop: the game idea

  **AirHop** is a single-player, browser-based 3D racing game. The pitch is "a car that sticks to any surface and spends a limited tank of compressed air on tricks." It's inspired by the compressed-air flying car Tesla showed in October 2026, but it uses an original car and original names.

  ## The core idea

  The car is a grasshopper, not a butterfly. Suction holds it to floors, walls and ceilings, so tracks can climb walls, run along ceilings and loop. Air is the resource that makes it a game.

  You get one shared **air tank of 100 units**, and everything special draws from it:

  | Move | Key | Cost |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hop (burst along the surface normal) | Space | 15 per hop |
  | Boost (forward thrust) | Hold Shift | 20 per second |
  | Air control (pitch, roll, yaw) | W/S, Q/E, A/D in the air | 3 per second |
  | Cushion burst (soft landing) | Space when descending near a surface | 5 |

  You earn air back in these ways:
  - **Flips:** +10 per full rotation.
  - **Perfect landing:** +5.
  - **Slow regeneration on surfaces:** +4 per second.
  - **Charge strips:** +40 per second.

  The central tension is spending air now to be faster versus saving it for a gap or tight corner later. A full tank is about 5 seconds of boost or 6 hops.

  ## The core loop

  1. Drive on any surface, held down by suction.
  2. Burn air to hop gaps, cut corners or boost.
  3. Flip in the air for bonus air, but land on the wheels.
  4. Refill slowly, or faster on charge strips.

  ## Landings

  Landings are the risk and reward part, judged by the angle between the car's up direction and the surface, plus impact speed:

  - **Perfect** (under 30° and under 8 m/s): +5 air and a small speed boost.
  - **Rough** (30–60°, or too fast): speed ×0.6.
  - **Crash** (over 60°): respawn at the last checkpoint after 1.5 s, plus a 3 s time penalty.

  The flip timing is tight on purpose. A clean flip takes about 1.4 s, and a hop gives about 1.8 s of airtime.

  ## What v1 contains

  - Desktop browser, keyboard controls, 60 fps target.
  - No backend. Best times are saved in `localStorage`.
  - Three tracks: *Warm-up* (gentle curves, one wall), *Spiral Tower* (climbing spiral with a ceiling) and *Gap Run* (hop-rewarding gaps and a loop).
  - A lap timer, a translucent ghost of your best lap, and 3 AI racers with skill factors of 0.85, 0.90 and 0.95.
  - A 3-lap race on each track.
  - Design rule: hops and flips are the fastest way through, but never the only way. Every track must be finishable with no hops, only slower.

  ## How it's built

  - **Stack:** TypeScript, Vite, Three.js, and Rapier physics (WebAssembly).
  - **Gravity:** Rapier's gravity is set to zero. The game applies its own, because "down" depends on which surface the car is on.
  - **Physics step:** fixed at 1/120 s, so the car behaves the same on slow and fast machines.
  - **Tracks:** closed Catmull-Rom splines. An up-vector frame is carried along the spline, then banking is added. A wall is 90° of banking and a ceiling is 180°.
  - **Camera:** a chase camera whose up-vector turns slowly toward the car's up, about 3 per second. This stops ceiling and loop driving from being nauseating. The field of view widens from 70° to 85° with speed.

  ## How the spec is organised

  The spec is written for Claude Code to build in five milestones:

  1. **Hop and land on flat ground.** This is the make-or-break "does it feel good" step.
  2. **Walls, ceilings and spline tracks.**
  3. **Racing, ghost and AI.**
  4. **Three real tracks, effects, sound and menus.**
  5. **Polish, performance and release.**

  Each milestone has measurable "Done when" checks, such as a top speed of 55–65 m/s and a hop apex of 4.1 m ± 0.4. It also has a tuning-constants table with sanity checks, a risk table (tunneling, jitter, camera sickness, loop twist) and a list of rules for Claude Code. The rules include strict TypeScript, no magic numbers, no allocations in the physics loop, a `?debug=1` mode, and a `DECISIONS.md` for ambiguities.

  ## Things to watch

  - **The risk is feel, not code.** Milestone 1 decides whether the game works, so playtesting there matters most.
  - **Suction versus gravity.** The spec sets σ = 1.5, so suction is 0.5 mg stronger than gravity on a ceiling. That margin is small, and tuning it will be fiddly.
  - **Spec inconsistencies.** The notation and tables are a bit loose in places. For example, boost uses `BOOST_ACCEL` = 20, but the "85 m/s with boost" check assumes 40 total. I'd also check the claim that the drag and throttle equations give ~60 m/s once suction and grip forces are included.

  - **Fog and sky:** exponential fog and a gradient or procedural sky hide the draw distance and add depth.
  - **Particles:** `three.quarks` or a custom `Points`/instanced-mesh system for thruster puffs, speed lines and landing dust.
  - **Trails:** a ribbon trail behind the car and the ghost helps convey speed and path.
  - **Custom shaders:** a scrolling shader on charge strips, a Fresnel glow on the ghost, and an animated grid on the track.

AirHop is a racing game where your car can drive on walls and ceilings, and where you manage one tank of compressed air.

  ## The goal

  You race three laps against three computer racers. Finish first, or beat your own best time. After you set a best lap, a see-through copy of it (the "ghost") races alongside you next time.

  ## The car

  - **It sticks to the road.** Suction holds the car to any surface, even upside down. That's why tracks can go up walls, across ceilings and around loops.
  - **You steer and accelerate** like a normal car: W to speed up, S to brake or reverse, A and D to steer.

  ## The air tank (the main idea)

  You have one tank of 100 air. Everything special costs air from it:

  | Move | Key | Cost |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hop (jump off the road) | Space | 15 |
  | Boost (extra speed) | Hold Shift | 20 per second |
  | Steering in the air | W/S, Q/E, A/D | 3 per second |
  | Soft landing | Space, just before landing | 5 |

  So you're always choosing: spend air now, or save it for later.

  ## How you get air back

  - **Slowly, just by driving:** 4 per second.
  - **Flips:** spin the car in the air one full turn, +10.
  - **Perfect landings:** +5.
  - **Charge strips on the track** (not built yet): +40 per second.

  ## Landings matter

  When you come back down, the game checks how level and how gentle you were:

  - **Perfect:** nearly level and not too hard. You get +5 air and a small speed boost.
  - **Rough:** a bit tilted or too hard. You lose 40% of your speed.
  - **Crash:** badly tilted. You respawn at the last checkpoint after 1.5 seconds and get a 3 second time penalty.

  ## Racing

  - A **3-2-1 countdown** starts the race.
  - **Checkpoint gates** are spread around the track. You must pass them in order for a lap to count, so you can't take shortcuts.
  - **Penalties** (crashes, falling off, pressing R to respawn) add 3 seconds to your time.
  - After you finish, you get a results screen with your place, laps and standings.

  ## Why hop and flip at all?

  The idea is that hops, boosts and flips are the fastest way past gaps and tight corners, but never the only way to finish. The skill is deciding when they're worth the air.
